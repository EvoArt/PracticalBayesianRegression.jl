Choose sampler settings

The defaults are Stan's and brms's: 4 chains of 2000 iterations, half of them warm-up, a target acceptance rate of 0.8, a maximum tree depth of 10 and a diagonal mass matrix. They suit most models. This page covers the settings to change when a fit reports problems or is slow.

Run chains in parallel

Chains run on separate threads. Start Julia with as many threads as chains:

julia -t 4

With one thread the chains run one after another. Check with Threads.nthreads().

Divergent transitions

A warning such as "There were 12 divergent transitions after warmup" means the sampler met regions of the posterior it could not follow, and the draws may be biased. Raise the target acceptance rate, which makes the sampler take smaller steps:

fit = brm(f, df; delta = 0.95)

If divergences remain, the model is usually the cause: weakly identified parameters, or a funnel between a group-level standard deviation and its effects when there are few groups or little data per group. Tighter priors on the standard deviations often help.

Maximum tree depth

If many iterations hit the maximum tree depth (see nuts_params(fit)["treedepth__"]), sampling is inefficient but not biased. Raise the limit with max_depth = 12; often reparameterising or rescaling the predictors helps more.

Dense mass matrix

During warm-up the sampler learns the scale of each parameter (a diagonal mass matrix). With metric = :dense it also learns their correlations, at the cost of more work per step:

fit = brm(f, df; metric = :dense)

Whether that pays off depends on the model. In comparisons of effective sample size per second over 19 models with up to about 100 parameters, five runs each:

Modeldense / diagonal
gaussian, random intercepts (50 groups)3.6
gaussian, random intercepts and slopes1.9
gaussian, crossed random intercepts2.3
Poisson, random intercepts3.0
binomial, random intercepts4.0
beta, random intercepts5.2
Student-t, distributional, zero-inflated, hurdle1.2 to 1.6
fixed effects only, few parametersabout 1
Bernoulli, random intercepts0.55
50 population-level coefficients0.59
ordinal, random interceptsabout 1

So:

  • Try metric = :dense for multilevel models with a moderate number of parameters and for models with correlated parameters (distributional models, families with several parameters).
  • Keep the default for models with many coefficients (with hundreds or thousands of parameters, the warm-up cannot estimate a dense matrix well and each step costs more), and for Bernoulli multilevel models.
  • When it matters, compare: fit both and look at the effective sample sizes and the sampling time printed with the summary.

Warm-up and number of draws

The defaults give 4000 draws, plenty for means and 95% intervals. For tail quantities or Bayes factors, run more iterations (iter = 4000). Thinning (thin) only discards information; use it to save memory.

Initial values

Chains start at values drawn uniformly from (-2, 2) on the unconstrained scale. If initialisation fails ("no finite log density in 100 random initialisations"), narrow the range with init = 0.5, or check the priors and data for impossible values.

Reproducibility

seed = 1 makes a fit reproducible: the same seed, data and settings give the same draws, with or without threads.

Other samplers

sampler = :advancedhmc uses AdvancedHMC.jl's NUTS on the same log density, and any AdvancedHMC.AbstractHMCSampler can be passed. The package's own NUTS is the default because it was faster in every comparison.
