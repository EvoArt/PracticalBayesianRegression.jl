Censored, truncated and weighted data

Information about the response beyond its value goes on the left-hand side of the formula, after |. Several addition terms combine with +: y | trials(n) + weights(w) ~ x.

Censoring

A censored observation is known only to lie beyond a limit, or within an interval. Give a censoring indicator with cens():

brm(@bf(time | cens(censored) ~ treatment), df; family = weibull())

censored holds, for each row, "none" (or 0, or false) for an exact value, "right" (1, true) when the true value is above time, "left" (-1) when it is below, and "interval" (2) when it lies between time and a second variable given as cens(censored, time_upper):

brm(@bf(y | cens(censored, y_upper) ~ x), df)

Censoring works for gaussian, lognormal, weibull and exponential responses and for the count families poisson, negbinomial, negbinomial2, geometric, binomial, beta_binomial, discrete_weibull and com_poisson. For survival times, cox also takes cens().

Truncation

A truncated response cannot be observed outside known bounds:

brm(@bf(y | trunc(lb = 0) ~ x), df)                       # y > 0
brm(@bf(count | trunc(lb = 1) ~ x), df; family = poisson())   # zero-truncated Poisson
brm(@bf(y | trunc(lb = 0, ub = 100) ~ x), df)

For count families the bounds are inclusive, as in Stan: trunc(lb = 1) allows 1, 2, ... Bounds may also be data columns: trunc(ub = ymax).

Weights

weights(w) multiplies each observation's log-likelihood by w:

brm(@bf(y | weights(w) ~ x), df)

Known standard errors

se(s) gives each observation a known standard error, as in a meta-analysis. With se(s, sigma = true) a residual standard deviation is estimated as well:

brm(@bf(effect | se(std_err) ~ 1 + (1 | study)), meta)

Trials and exposure

trials(n) is the number of trials of a binomial-type response; rate(t) gives a count model an exposure t (an offset of log(t) on the mean):

brm(@bf(successes | trials(n) ~ x), df; family = binomial())
brm(@bf(cases | rate(person_years) ~ x), df; family = poisson())
