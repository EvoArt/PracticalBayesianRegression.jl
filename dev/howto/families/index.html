Choose a family and link

The family is the distribution of the response given the predictors. Pass it to brm with family = .... Each family has a default link for its mean mu; choose another with link:

brm(@bf(y ~ x), df; family = poisson())
brm(@bf(y ~ x), df; family = bernoulli(link = :probit))
brm(@bf(y ~ x), df; family = "negbinomial")      # a brms family name also works

Each family's reference entry lists its parameters and links.

Links

The link connects the linear predictor to mu: mu = linkinv(eta). The defaults are those of brms: identity for gaussian-type families, log for counts and positive responses, logit for probabilities. Other links:

bernoulli(link = :probit)
poisson(link = :sqrt)
gammafamily(link = :identity)
cumulative(link = :cloglog)

The available names are identity, log, inverse, sqrt, inverse_squared, logit, probit, probit_approx, cloglog, cauchit, softit, softplus, squareplus and tan_half; each family accepts the subset brms accepts for it. An unsupported link raises an error that lists the family's links.

gammafamily() defaults to the log link, while brms's Gamma() defaults to the inverse link. Write gammafamily(link = :inverse) to match a brms model that relies on the default.

Other parameters

The other parameters of a family (sigma, shape, phi, zi, ...) have fixed links: log for positive parameters, logit for probabilities. Model any of them with a formula, as in A distributional model:

brm(@bf(y ~ x, shape ~ x), df; family = negbinomial())
brm(@bf(y ~ x, zi ~ z), df; family = zero_inflated_poisson())

Mixtures

mixture combines families into a finite mixture. Each component has its own predictors (mu1, mu2, ...) and the mixing proportions are theta1, theta2, ...:

brm(@bf(y ~ x), df; family = mixture(gaussian(), gaussian()))
brm(@bf(y ~ 1, mu2 ~ x), df; family = mixture(gaussian(), student()))

When all components are of the same family, the component intercepts are ordered so that the components are identified, as brms does by default.

Your own family

custom_family defines a family from a log density written in Julia, as brms's custom_family does from Stan code.
