Ordinal and categorical responses

Ordered categories

Ratings, grades and other ordered responses use an ordinal family. The response is an integer 1, 2, ..., K, or a categorical vector whose levels are in order (a CategoricalArray keeps its own level order; plain strings are sorted).

fit = brm(@bf(rating ~ treatment + (1 | rater)), df; family = cumulative())

The model has a latent continuous variable cut at K - 1 ordered thresholds, reported as b_Intercept[1], ..., b_Intercept[K-1]. The population-level coefficients shift the latent variable, so a positive coefficient moves responses towards the higher categories.

posterior_epred returns the probability of each category: an array of draws x observations x categories.

p = posterior_epred(fit)
size(p)                 # (ndraws, nobs, K)

Which ordinal family

  • cumulative: one latent variable and thresholds; the usual choice.
  • sratio, cratio: sequential processes, where a response passes through the categories in turn.
  • acat: adjacent-category logits.

All take the links logit (default), probit, probit_approx, cloglog and cauchit; cumulative and acat also softit.

Category-specific effects

With sratio, cratio and acat, cs(x) gives x a separate effect at each threshold (brms does not allow this for cumulative):

brm(@bf(rating ~ cs(treatment) + age), df; family = acat())

Thresholds

thres(gr = g) estimates a separate set of thresholds for each level of g; thres(K - 1) fixes the number of thresholds when not every category is observed:

brm(@bf(rating | thres(gr = rater) ~ treatment), df; family = cumulative())
brm(@bf(rating | thres(6) ~ treatment), df; family = cumulative())

Discrimination

A formula for disc models the scale of the latent variable:

brm(@bf(rating ~ treatment, disc ~ 0 + group), df; family = cumulative())

As in brms, disc is fixed at 1 unless it has a formula. Leave out the intercept (0 + ...) or fix it, since the overall scale is not identified.

Monotonic predictors

An ordinal predictor (education level, dose category) can enter with mo(x): its effect increases or decreases monotonically across the categories, with free step sizes:

brm(@bf(y ~ mo(education) + age), df)

The coefficient bsp_moeducation is the average change per category; the simplex simo_moeducation1 holds the share of the total change at each step.

Unordered categories

categorical is the multinomial logit model. The response has K categories; the first is the reference, and each other category gets its own linear predictor, mu2, ..., muK:

fit = brm(@bf(choice ~ price), df; family = categorical())

Give a category its own formula with mu3 ~ .... posterior_epred returns the category probabilities, as for ordinal families.

Counts or proportions in several categories

When each observation is a vector of counts (or proportions) over K categories, give the columns with cbind:

brm(@bf(cbind(a, b, c) ~ x), df; family = multinomial())       # counts
brm(@bf(cbind(pa, pb, pc) ~ x), df; family = dirichlet())       # proportions summing to 1
brm(@bf(cbind(pa, pb, pc) ~ x), df; family = logistic_normal())

Every category but the first gets a predictor named after its column (mub, muc).
