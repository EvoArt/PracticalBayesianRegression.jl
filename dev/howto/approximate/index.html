Use approximate inference

NUTS gives exact draws from the posterior, given enough of them. For a quick first look at a large model, or to get starting values, an approximation can be enough. Choose one with algorithm, as in brms:

fit = brm(f, df; algorithm = :laplace)
fit = brm(f, df; algorithm = :pathfinder, ndraws = 2000)
algorithmMethod
:samplingNUTS (the default)
:laplacea normal approximation at the posterior mode on the unconstrained scale, with the Hessian at the mode
:meanfieldvariational inference (ADVI) with independent normal factors
:fullrankvariational inference with a full-covariance normal
:pathfinderPathfinder: normal approximations along an optimisation path, the best one resampled by importance weights

The result is an ordinary fit with ndraws draws (1000 by default) in one chain, so summaries, predictions and plots work as usual. R-hat and effective sample sizes are not meaningful for these draws.

The approximations are computed on the unconstrained scale. They tend to understate the uncertainty when the posterior is skewed or has strong dependence between parameters, as in multilevel models with few groups. Check important results against a NUTS fit.
